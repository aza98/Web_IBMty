# Perception and Visual Hierarchy

Concept summaries, with applications and checks proposed by this skill. Read for layouts, components, states, and visual relationships.

## Aesthetic-Usability Effect

An attractive appearance can improve perceived ease of use and conceal usability problems.

**Apply:** refine typography, rhythm, alignment, and brand consistency alongside task completion.

**Limit:** aesthetic satisfaction does not establish effectiveness. Do not accept an interface because it looks polished if the task fails.

**Check:** assess aesthetic opinions, completion, errors, and observed effort separately.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/aesthetic-usability-effect/)

## Law of Common Region

A shared boundary or background suggests membership in a group.

**Apply:** delimit genuine groups, such as an order summary and its actions.

**Limit:** nested cards can fragment a task and add noise. Use a container only when the relationship needs one.

**Check:** verify that actions or data from one group are not attributed to another.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-common-region/)

## Law of Proximity

Nearby elements tend to be perceived as related.

**Apply:** keep related labels, fields, help, and errors close together; distinguish spacing within a group from spacing between groups.

**Limit:** visual association does not replace an accessible semantic relationship. Appropriate spacing depends on content and device.

**Check:** review associations on mobile, with long text, and under magnification, as well as in the initial layout.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-proximity/)

## Law of Prägnanz

Perception tends to organize complex or ambiguous shapes into simple structures.

**Apply:** use distinguishable silhouettes, alignment, and groupings that are easy to interpret.

**Limit:** a minimal symbol can be ambiguous. Do not remove necessary text or impose minimalism as a mandatory style.

**Check:** identify shapes, actions, and relationships without relying on decorative details or external explanations.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-pr%C3%A4gnanz/)

## Law of Similarity

Visual resemblance communicates a relationship or shared function.

**Apply:** use consistent treatments for equivalent actions; distinguish links, controls, and static content.

**Limit:** a shared color should not falsely imply identical behavior. Do not communicate meaning through color alone.

**Check:** review whether people can predict which elements are interactive and which serve the same purpose.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-similarity/)

## Law of Uniform Connectedness

An explicit visual connection reinforces the relationship between elements.

**Apply:** use lines, paths, or connectors for sequences, dependencies, and diagrams when the relationship is real.

**Limit:** a decorative line can suggest a nonexistent dependency. Do not confuse a connected appearance with accessible reading order.

**Check:** verify that the user can follow the relationships and that they remain understandable in an alternative layout.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/law-of-uniform-connectedness/)

## Selective Attention

Attention prioritizes a subset of stimuli, usually related to the current goal.

**Apply:** place feedback and errors near the action that triggers them. Avoid making important notices resemble advertisements or compete with simultaneous changes.

**Limit:** something visible can go unnoticed. More animation or greater size does not guarantee understanding.

**Check:** while the task is being performed, observe whether the relevant change is detected without pointing it out beforehand.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/selective-attention/)

## Von Restorff Effect

An element that differs from an otherwise similar group may be remembered better.

**Apply:** reserve emphasis for priority actions or information and keep alternatives clear.

**Limit:** when everything stands out, differentiation becomes less useful. Remembering a button does not prove it is understood or is the appropriate choice; avoid relying on color or motion alone.

**Check:** ask people to identify the primary action and its consequence, and review differentiation with reduced motion and without color cues.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/von-restorff-effect/)
