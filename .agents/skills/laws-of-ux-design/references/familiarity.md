# Familiarity and Learning

Concept summaries, with applications and checks proposed by this skill. Read when changing patterns, labels, navigation, or onboarding.

## Jakob’s Law

Prior experiences create expectations about similar interfaces. Identify the products and conventions familiar to the audience, not just to the designer.

**Apply:** preserve the meaning of links, buttons, search, back navigation, and states. Reuse product components and platform patterns. An original visual direction can preserve these conventions. For a major change, consider a gradual transition or preview when cost and context allow.

**Limit:** popularity does not establish suitability or accessibility. A new pattern can be useful when its benefit outweighs learning costs; do not require maintaining two versions by default.

**Check:** observe whether a person identifies the action and anticipates its result without explanation; compare errors and initial learning.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/jakobs-law/)

## Mental Model

People act according to their explanation of the system, which may differ from its actual architecture.

**Apply:** organize navigation and vocabulary around audience tasks. Clearly explain what is saved, shared, or deleted, and where results appear.

**Limit:** do not infer a mental model solely from a competitor or your own experience. Label assumptions when research is unavailable.

**Check:** ask people to predict what will happen before acting; compare the prediction with the result. Use interviews or card sorting when the uncertainty concerns the underlying structure.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/mental-model/)

## Paradox of the Active User

Many people prefer starting their task to learning the system through documentation.

**Apply:** enable a first useful result with contextual help, examples, and action-oriented empty states. Keep help available after the introduction is skipped.

**Limit:** do not interpret this as meaning nobody reads. Preserve necessary instructions and avoid placing critical information only in a tooltip.

**Check:** attempt the first task while skipping the tour and entering through a secondary screen; verify that help remains accessible.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/paradox-of-the-active-user/)
