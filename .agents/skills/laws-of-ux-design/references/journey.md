# Progress, Recall, and Prioritization

Concept summaries, with applications and checks proposed by this skill. Read for long journeys, confirmations, onboarding, and prioritizing improvements.

## Flow

Sustained concentration is associated with clear goals, useful feedback, and a challenge matched to ability.

**Apply:** preserve the working context, communicate results, and reduce interruptions unrelated to the task. Provide help for beginners and shortcuts for experienced users where appropriate.

**Limit:** a fast task does not establish a flow state. Do not confuse concentration with maximizing time spent.

**Check:** observe interruptions and recovery of context, distinguishing participants with different experience levels.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/flow/)

## Goal-Gradient Effect

Perceived proximity to a goal can increase motivation to complete it.

**Apply:** show completed and remaining steps using an understandable basis, and communicate the next useful milestone.

**Limit:** do not fabricate progress or hide remaining work. If steps involve different amounts of effort, avoid representing their count as an exact percentage of effort.

**Check:** verify whether the person understands what remains and whether the indicator matches the actual state.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/goal-gradient-effect/)

## Peak-End Rule

Intense moments and the ending can have a particularly strong influence on how an experience is remembered.

**Apply:** attend to moments of uncertainty, error recovery, and confirmations. At completion, show what happened, what is retained, and what comes next.

**Limit:** a celebration does not compensate for a defective journey. Do not use this rule to justify earlier friction or deliberately prolong the experience.

**Check:** assess incidents during the journey and later recall separately, including the ending after a failure.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/peak-end-rule/)

## Serial Position Effect

The first and last items in a sequence may be remembered better.

**Apply:** consider the placement of important information in sequences where recall matters, preserving logical order and expectations.

**Limit:** do not reorganize stable navigation just to put actions at the ends; memory, discoverability, and clicks are different outcomes.

**Check:** observe recall and findability separately; review whether the sequence still makes sense.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/serial-position-effect/)

## Zeigarnik Effect

Interrupted or incomplete tasks can remain more prominent in memory.

**Apply:** support saving drafts, identifying pending work, and resuming from the previous state.

**Limit:** the effect depends on context and does not guarantee retention. Do not leave tasks artificially incomplete or create anxiety to attract return visits.

**Check:** interrupt and resume a task; verify data preservation and clarity of the next step. Distinguish recall from motivation.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/zeigarnik-effect/)

## Pareto Principle

Impact can be concentrated in a subset of causes or tasks.

**Apply:** prioritize improvements using frequency, impact, and effort data; identify journeys whose failure particularly affects the product's goal.

**Limit:** 80/20 is a heuristic reference, not a demonstrated distribution within the product. An infrequent task can be critical; do not dismiss accessibility or recovery because of low volume.

**Check:** document the source of the data. Without metrics, present prioritization as provisional and explain how to revisit it.

[Source](https://lawsofux.com/pareto-principle/)
